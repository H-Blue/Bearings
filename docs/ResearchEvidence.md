# Bearings research evidence and pitch reference

Prepared October 11, 2026. A targeted literature review for the owner's emotional-learning direction, covering 37 research papers, evidence reviews, frameworks and population/employer reports. This is a research reference, not a formal systematic review or an efficacy study of Bearings. It supplements [PitchResearch.md](PitchResearch.md) and the proposed [EmotionalLearningSpec.md](EmotionalLearningSpec.md).

## What the evidence supports

Bearings has a credible research-informed direction: help a person describe feelings precisely, reflect on context, question an interpretation, choose a small practice and review the outcome. Evidence is strongest for structured emotional-skills education and specific reflection/action techniques. Evidence is weaker for expecting a short vocabulary exercise, an AI interpretation or ordinary unstructured journaling to produce the same outcomes.

The best pitch connects three separate claims: there is a substantial need for connection/support; emotional skills can be learned; Bearings makes opportunities to practice those skills accessible in a private journal. The third statement describes the product's intended design and must be demonstrated in the app. Research on other programs does not establish that Bearings reduces loneliness, depression, anger, or distress, or increases empathy, creativity or emotional intelligence.

Use one need statistic and one learning result in a short presentation. Keep the rest for the submission and questions. The source cards below supply direct links and boundaries.

## How to interpret the numbers

Percentages describing a population are different from percentage changes in an outcome. Correlations describe associations: r = .30 does not mean 30% improvement. Standardized effects such as d, g and SMD express differences in standard-deviation units: SMD = .46 does not mean 46% improvement. Partial eta squared is an effect-size statistic, not the fraction of negative emotion removed. Statistical significance does not itself show a clinically important change.

Meta-analyses are broader than single studies, but depend on the quality and compatibility of their included studies. A randomized trial better supports causation for its tested intervention and outcome; an observational association does not establish that training the skill causes the associated benefit. Some reviews below share primary studies; their sample counts must not be added as independent evidence.

## Need: connection, support and difficulty describing feelings

### [01] WHO Commission on Social Connection, 2025

**Design/finding:** WHO estimates that 15.8% of people globally, approximately one in six, report loneliness. It distinguishes subjective loneliness from objective social isolation.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports the problem framing for a feature that helps people reach a trusted person. Emotional literacy could be one helpful capability, but this report does not establish that a vocabulary gap causes loneliness or that Bearings reduces it. Use the report year; this is an estimate rather than a real-time population count. [WHO source](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/social-connection).

### [02] CDC: loneliness and social/emotional support, 2024

**Design/finding:** Analysis of 2022 BRFSS data from 26 U.S. states, overall analytic sample 218,915. Among adults aged 18–34, 29.7% received needed social/emotional support sometimes, rarely or never; 43.3% reported loneliness under the study definition. Overall adult lack-of-support prevalence was 24.1%.

**Relevance and limit:** Strong population context for a young-adult story. The survey is observational, self-reported and restricted to participating states. These percentages do not measure emotional neglect or low EI. [Original CDC report](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/73/wr/mm7324a1.htm).

### [03] CDC/NCHS: U.S. teenagers' perceived support, 2024

**Design/finding:** Nationally representative 2021–2022 teen survey analysis, ages 12–17, n = 1,157. Some 58.5% reported always/usually receiving needed support. The calculated complement is approximately 41.5% who did not report consistent support at that level.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports an emotional-support need in adolescence, if discussing that audience. This does not establish neglect, diagnose a skill deficit or validate offering the prototype to minors. The percentage describes the survey's support question. [Original report](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/nhsr/nhsr206.pdf).

### [04] Taillieu et al., 2016: childhood emotional maltreatment

**Design/finding:** Retrospective analysis of nationally representative 2004–2005 U.S. data from 34,653 adults aged 20+. Emotional neglect alone was 6.2%; neglect together with emotional abuse was 3.1%. Combining the mutually exclusive categories gives approximately 9.3% with childhood emotional neglect under this study's threshold.

**Relevance and limit:** Directly addresses the owner's earlier neglect question. Old retrospective data is less useful for the main pitch than current connection/support reporting. It cannot tell us how many people currently need Bearings. [Original paper](https://grn.issup.net/files/2018-02/1-s2.0-S0145213416301442-main.pdf).

### [05] Ditzer et al., 2023: maltreatment and alexithymia

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 99 independent samples from 78 sources, 36,141 participants. Childhood maltreatment was associated with adult alexithymia, difficulty identifying/describing emotions: r = .23, 95% CI .19–.27. Emotional neglect specifically had r = .21, CI .16–.26.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports taking difficulties naming feelings seriously and using gentle, nonjudgmental prompts. Correlation does not establish causation or show that a journal reverses effects of neglect. Do not infer a user's history from their vocabulary. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37261746/).

## Emotional Zoom: differentiation, labeling and learning

### [06] Hong et al., 2025: differentiation and depressive symptoms

**Design/finding:** Systematic review/meta-analysis of 16 samples, 2,515 participants. Negative emotion differentiation had a small association with fewer depressive symptoms, r = −.14, 95% CI −.21 to −.06. Positive differentiation was not significantly associated, r = −.02, CI −.07 to .03.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports studying precision around difficult feelings. The modest association and null positive result argue against sweeping mental-health claims. These are correlations, not treatment effects; abstract-level evidence was available from the publisher. [Original review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-07195-8).

### [07] Seah and Coifman, 2022: differentiation and behavior

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 17 studies found higher negative emotion differentiation associated with less maladaptive behavior, r = −.15. Across 11 studies controlling for negative affect, the association remained smaller, r = −.09.

**Relevance and limit:** Provides a rationale for differentiating feelings before choosing a response. Effects are small, the studies are heterogeneous, and the evidence does not show Bearings prevents harmful behavior. Do not turn this into an automatic risk prediction or claim that naming a feeling removes it. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34264705/).

### [08] Ozomaro et al., 2025: granularity across health research

**Design/finding:** A scoping review mapped 98 peer-reviewed articles across biological correlates, psychological functioning, development, clinical populations and interventions. The literature commonly linked finer emotional granularity with better functioning.

**Relevance and limit:** Useful overview showing this is an established research topic. A scoping review maps evidence rather than supplying one pooled causal effect. Clinical/nonclinical differences do not prove that low granularity causes a disorder. Detailed methods were not independently audited here; the publisher's abstract and accessible sections were inspected. [Original review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022395625004881).

### [09] Vedernikova, Kuppens and Erbas, 2021: five-day education trial

**Design/finding:** Randomized study of 120 adults, 60 per condition, using emotion education versus country knowledge. Negative emotion differentiation on a scenario-rating task improved after five days: training mean −1.39 to −1.15; control −1.39 to −1.41. Time-by-condition p < .001; partial eta squared .174. An exploratory 103-person follow-up found improvement still detectable one month later, with some decline.

**Relevance and limit:** Direct support for emotion education. The transformed score cannot give a meaningful percent improvement; .174 is not 17.4% less negative emotion. Positive differentiation did not show a significant intervention advantage. Mostly UK/White participants, hypothetical scenarios and inconsistent wellbeing findings limit transfer. [Full paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.703757/full).

### [10] Matt, Seah and Coifman, 2024: a useful null result

**Design/finding:** Randomized brief online emotion-word learning study, 118 retained college participants from 150 recruited; individual analyses used smaller samples. Two days of emotion-word learning did not produce significant overall differences in negative differentiation, emotional self-efficacy or distress versus neutral-word learning. Some exploratory engagement findings were promising.

**Relevance and limit:** A vocabulary list is insufficient evidence of benefit. Bearings should teach contextual distinctions and help users apply them, while evaluating whether that works. Exploratory subgroup/engagement findings should not be presented as a successful overall trial. [Full paper](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0299540).

### [11] Hoemann, Barrett and Quigley, 2021: repeated assessment

**Design/finding:** Fifty healthy young adults completed approximately 14 days of intensive experience sampling, physiological monitoring and end-of-day diaries across three to four weeks. Positive and negative granularity increased during assessment; exploratory analyses linked engagement and individual factors with change.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports investigating repeated noticing and describing, rather than one-time label generation. There was no randomized no-assessment comparison establishing that journaling alone caused improvement. The intensive, compensated research protocol differs substantially from ordinary app use. [Full paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.704125/full).

### [12] Lieberman et al., 2007: putting feelings into words

**Design/finding:** An fMRI experiment found that labeling affect in negative emotional images reduced amygdala/other limbic responses relative to comparison encoding conditions, with increased right ventrolateral prefrontal activity.

**Relevance and limit:** Laboratory mechanism evidence for affect labeling, relevant to inviting users to name feelings. Neural response to images is not equivalent to sustained everyday calm or clinical recovery. This study does not validate AI choosing an emotion for a person, or a percentage reduction in distress. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17576282/) and [author-hosted paper](https://www.semel.ucla.edu/sites/default/files/publications/May%202007%20-%20Putting%20Feelings%20Into%20Words.pdf).

## Emotional education: adults, workplaces and Yale

### [13] Hodzic et al., 2018: adult EI training

**Design/finding:** Multilevel meta-analysis of 24 studies, 28 samples, examining EI training in healthy adults. The publisher abstract reports a moderate pre/post improvement and a stable pre/follow-up effect, moderated by model, dimension, training length and publication type.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports the proposition that emotional skills are trainable. Pre/post change alone is weaker than an active-control comparison, and programs/measures differ. The complete primary paper was not available through the publisher here, so use the qualitative abstract claim rather than a precise numerical estimate. [Original review](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1754073917708613).

### [14] Durham et al., 2023: structured online adult learning

**Design/finding:** Randomized comparison with a matched active placebo program. Of 448 initial participants, 326 had usable post-training data: 168 EI training, 158 control; 72% female, average age approximately 24. Programs involved 10–12 hours across one or three weeks. EI training improved self-report and performance-based measures relative to control.

**Relevance and limit:** Particularly relevant to digital lessons. This was a substantial reviewed curriculum, not a few generated tips. Attrition was 27%; excluded participants had lower baseline EI. Only 91 completed six-month follow-up, disrupted by COVID-19; the performance-score increase versus baseline was marginal, p = .083, and self-report gains were not maintained. [Full paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1221817/full).

### [15] Mehler et al., 2024: workplace competencies

**Design/finding:** Preregistered systematic review of workplace EI, empathy and regulation training. Fifty studies contributed pre/post analysis; 27 controlled trials contributed intervention/control analysis. Controlled effect SMD = .46, 95% CI .30–.63; pre/post SMD = .44. The review reports benefits persisting beyond three months.

**Relevance and limit:** Stronger support for a workplace emotional-skills narrative than speculative claims about AI replacing coding. The authors flag high heterogeneity and low methodological quality. These are standardized effects, not percentage improvements or estimates for Bearings. [Full review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40359-024-02198-3).

### [16] Yale's RULER framework

**Framework:** Recognizing, Understanding, Labeling, Expressing and Regulating emotions. It connects noticing and vocabulary with understanding, expression and regulation in a school-wide approach.

**Relevance and limit:** Informs the proposed learning sequence. Bearings could describe its design as informed by this research without copying a proprietary curriculum or suggesting endorsement. RULER is a framework/program, not an independent trial result; research below tests particular implementations. A self-guided journal differs from a trained-teacher school environment. [Official Yale overview](https://medicine.yale.edu/childstudy/services/community-and-schools-programs/center-for-emotional-intelligence/ruler/).

### [17] Brackett et al., 2012: RULER Feeling Words Curriculum

**Design/finding:** Thirty-week quasi-experimental study of 273 fifth/sixth graders in 15 classrooms across three schools. RULER classrooms had better year-end grades and teacher-rated social/emotional competence than comparison classrooms.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports sustained teaching of emotion concepts with practice. Nonrandom assignment, a small number of schools and teacher-rated outcomes constrain inference. It does not demonstrate adult app effects, empathy gains or a benefit from AI. The Yale-hosted study abstract was inspected. [Yale study record](https://isps.yale.edu/resource/enhancing-academic-performance-and-social-and-emotional-competence-with-the-ruler-feeling).

### [18] Rivers et al., 2013: randomized RULER implementation

**Design/finding:** Cluster randomized trial in 62 schools found improvements in classroom-climate features, including warmth/connectedness, student autonomy/leadership and attention to student interests.

**Relevance and limit:** Stronger causal support for a school-wide emotional-learning implementation. Classroom observations concern an educational environment; they are not individual empathy scores or effects of journaling. Preserve that distinction when explaining why the app encourages emotionally literate communication. The publisher abstract and study record were inspected. [Original study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11121-012-0305-2).

### [19] Cipriano et al., 2023: contemporary SEL evidence

**Design/finding:** Yale-led systematic review/meta-analysis of 424 studies from 53 countries, covering 252 school-based interventions and 575,361 K–12 students. Compared with controls, participants improved across skills, attitudes, behavior, climate/safety, peer relationships, school functioning and achievement.

**Relevance and limit:** The strongest broad Yale-linked education reference in this brief. Outcomes varied with content, context and implementation quality. Programs involve more than labeling and usually human educational support. It supports SEL generally, not Yale endorsement or a validated adult app. [Original review](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cdev.13968).

### [20] Durlak et al., 2011: foundational SEL meta-analysis

**Design/finding:** Review of 213 school-based programs, 270,034 students. Compared with controls, SEL participants improved in emotional/social skills, attitudes, behavior and academic achievement; the authors describe an 11-percentile-point achievement gain.

**Relevance and limit:** Useful foundational evidence for teaching and applying emotional skills. Eleven percentile points is not an 11% EI increase and should not become a predicted gain for Bearings. School results are indirect for adults and for a private journal. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21291449/).

## Journaling, perspective and editable assumptions

### [21] Sohal et al., 2022: journaling review

**Design/finding:** Systematic review/meta-analysis of 20 randomized trials, 31 outcomes: 17 expressive-writing and three gratitude-journaling studies. The authors report an approximately 5% greater score reduction relative to control under their pooling method, with small-to-moderate standardized benefits.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports journaling as a possible adjunct, but heterogeneity and methodological flaws prevent a confident universal effect estimate. Clinical significance across the different scales was unclear. Prefer saying journaling has some supportive trial evidence over advertising “5% less negative emotion.” It also does not establish the effects of AI analysis. [Full review](https://fmch.bmj.com/content/10/1/e001154).

### [22] Kross and Ayduk, 2008: distanced reflection

**Design/finding:** Experiments comparing reflection on negative personal experiences from a distanced perspective with immersed analysis/distraction found benefits for emotional responses. In the second study, distanced analysis also reduced recurring thoughts and depressed affect at later assessments, including one and seven days.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports asking what a caring observer might notice and helping a writer reconstruct meaning. Laboratory instructions are not equivalent to a personalized AI conversation. A user should be able to pause rather than repeatedly replay a painful event. [Original paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0146167208315938).

### [23] Ayduk and Kross, 2010: spontaneous self-distancing

**Design/finding:** Three studies linked greater spontaneous self-distancing with less emotional/cardiovascular reactivity, fewer intrusive thoughts over time, and more constructive interpersonal behavior. The pattern reflected reconstruing an experience rather than simply recounting it, not avoidance.

**Relevance and limit:** Reinforces the distinction between useful reflection and rumination. This research on spontaneous differences is substantially observational; it does not prove an app prompt produces the same effects. It supports a design hypothesis for brief perspective prompts. [Full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2881638/).

### [24] Webb, Miles and Sheeran, 2012: emotion-regulation techniques

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 306 experimental comparisons. Cognitive-change approaches had a small-to-moderate overall effect on emotional outcomes, d = .36; individual techniques and contexts varied. Reappraising a stimulus and perspective-taking generally outperformed some other strategies.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports exploring alternative interpretations and selecting a response. Emotional regulation includes more than positive thinking; reappraisal should not deny a real wrong or erase a boundary. Effects differ by technique/outcome and are not percentage reductions. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22582737/).

## Difficult moments: pausing and helpful memories

### [25] Balban et al., 2023: five-minute breathing practice

**Design/finding:** Remote randomized trial, 108 participants, comparing three five-minute daily breathing practices with mindfulness meditation for approximately one month. Exhalation-focused cyclic sighing improved positive affect more than mindfulness and reduced respiratory rate more over time.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports offering a reviewed, optional breathing exercise. This was repeated practice, not evidence that a single tap instantly calms everyone. Negative affect and state anxiety declined across conditions without the same clear between-group advantage. Don't transfer results to any arbitrary breathing animation. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895).

### [26] Speer and Delgado, 2017: positive memory recall

**Design/finding:** Randomized laboratory behavioral study, final n = 134, and an additional fMRI study, n = 43. Recalling positive rather than neutral autobiographical memories after an acute stressor buffered cortisol increases and negative affect.

**Relevance and limit:** Direct rationale for a user-selected “something that helped” memory library. These were young, healthy participants, validated memories and controlled acute stress; an app has not demonstrated equivalent effects. Recollection should be voluntary and user-chosen rather than assuming every positive-looking entry is comforting. [Full paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6719713/).

## Weekly practices and explicit outcome reviews

### [27] Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006: specific if–then plans

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 94 independent tests reported a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment, d = .65, for implementation intentions: linking a specified situation to a specified action.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports converting a lesson into an actionable plan: “If plans change, I will explain that I was disappointed and ask for a workable time.” Evidence spans many goals; it does not directly validate emotionally adaptive weekly AI lessons or this exact wording. [Original review](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1) and [author's university record](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes).

### [28] Harkin et al., 2016: monitoring progress

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 138 randomized studies, 19,951 participants, found that interventions encouraging progress monitoring improved goal attainment, d = .40, CI .32–.48. Recording progress was one moderator of larger effects.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports a saved, explicit “What happened when you tried it?” review. The study did not test the proposed weekly lesson algorithm or justify inferring success from sentiment. Some public-reporting interventions had larger effects, but the private app need not copy that element. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26479070/).

## Empathy and reaching another person

### [29] Erbas et al., 2016: differentiation and empathic accuracy

**Design/finding:** Study of 98 adults in 49 couples. More differentiated negative emotional experience was associated with better estimates of a partner's emotional valence, r = .30. Results did not extend uniformly to positive differentiation or arousal accuracy.

**Relevance and limit:** Links self-understanding with one facet of understanding another person. Observational association does not establish that granularity training increases empathy. A partner's motives still need clarification; the app should suggest a question rather than claim to know them. [Author-hosted original paper](https://www.ppw.kuleuven.be/okp/_pdf/Erbas2016FMFYT.pdf).

### [30] Teding van Berkhout and Malouff, 2016: empathy training

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 18 randomized trials after excluding an outlier, 1,018 participants. Empathy training showed an overall g = .63; a publication-bias adjustment reduced it to .51. Training conditions, populations and assessment choices mattered.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports treating empathy as a skill that can be practiced. Those programs are not synonymous with emotion vocabulary or journaling. Future perspective/listening exercises need their own evaluation. The correction listed with the article concerns author-name formatting. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26191979/).

### [31] Kahlon et al., 2021: empathetic telephone calls

**Design/finding:** Randomized trial with 240 Meals on Wheels clients during COVID-19. Four weeks of empathy-focused calls from trained laypeople improved UCLA loneliness scores versus no calls, d = .48, and improved depression/anxiety measures. A second loneliness scale did not show a statistically significant intervention effect.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports facilitating real human conversation. Older/homebound clients and an organized repeated-call program differ from a friend callback request. A message-sending feature cannot claim the same result or guarantee someone responds. [Full trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2776786).

### [32] Liu et al., online 2022 / journal issue 2023: reaching out

**Design/finding:** A series of preregistered experiments found people underestimated how much others appreciated being contacted, especially when contact was more surprising. The research concerns checking in through messages or small gifts.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports a gentle, voluntary invitation to contact someone. Appreciation is different from access to care or reduction in loneliness, and these experiments do not establish that a distressed person can safely contact any acquaintance. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35816566/).

## Work, coding and creativity

### [33] World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025

**Design/finding:** Employer survey places creative thinking fourth and motivation/self-awareness fifth among core skills; empathy/active listening also appears in the top ten. Technology-related skills remain important and are also expected to grow.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports the owner's line about human understanding and collaboration remaining valuable as AI changes work. Employer expectations are not experimental evidence that Bearings improves employability, or that coding expertise is being eliminated. [Original skills outlook](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/).

### [34] Xu et al., 2019: EI and creativity

**Design/finding:** Meta-analysis of 75 studies, 96 correlations, 18,130 participants: overall EI/creativity correlation r = .32, CI .26–.38. The relationship depended strongly on measurement. Ability EI had a nonsignificant r = .08; divergent-thinking tests had a much smaller r = .07.

**Relevance and limit:** Creativity is a possible future research direction, not a demonstrated product benefit. The stronger overall result largely reflects subjective measures; shared response tendencies and high heterogeneity matter. Do not pitch “32% more creative.” [Original paper PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sustainability/sustainability-11-06123/article_deploy/sustainability-11-06123.pdf).

### [35] The well-being of software engineers, online 2024 / issue 2025

**Design/finding:** Systematic review synthesized 44 quantitative survey studies from 2000–2023, representing 16,086 software engineers. It models wellbeing through individual, team and organizational factors and connects it with professional functioning.

**Relevance and limit:** Provides profession-specific context for treating developers as people working in teams. Survey research cannot prove that an EI app improves coding productivity. Do not turn a general wellbeing review into proof that AI outsourcing makes emotional skills the only remaining valuable expertise. [Original review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10664-024-10543-8).

## Private AI assistance: evidence and boundaries

### [36] Patients' perspectives on mHealth privacy, 2024

**Design/finding:** Systematic review of 33 articles identified privacy, confidentiality and security concerns, awareness gaps, adoption barriers/facilitators and associated factors. Users wanted personal-data protection and understandable assurances.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports Bearings' local data boundary and clear explanations about what AI reads. The review covers varied health apps and did not perform study-quality appraisal. It does not prove local processing alone creates trust or provides complete security; backups, access controls and data flows must be described accurately. [Full review](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e50715).

### [37] Moore et al., 2025: limits of general-purpose LLM therapy

**Design/finding:** FAccT research evaluated models' attitudes and responses in mental-health scenarios. Tested systems expressed stigma and gave inappropriate responses to some important situations, including reinforcing delusional thinking.

**Relevance and limit:** Supports keeping generated interpretations tentative, preserving user correction, using reviewed educational material and offering access to people. The tested models/tasks are not Apple's on-device model or Bearings, so their exact performance cannot be transferred. This is a design rationale for evaluating our own model behavior, not evidence of app efficacy. [Published conference paper](https://facctconference.org/static/docs/facct2025-206archivalpdfs/facct2025-final197-acmpaginated.pdf).

## What this means for the proposed features

These are design inferences, not new measured findings:

| Feature | Evidence connection | Concrete design choice | What still needs evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Emotional Zoom | [09–12] | Ask for the person's words; offer nearby distinctions with examples; allow multiple/custom/uncertain answers | Can users apply a distinction to a new situation? |
| Editable assumptions | [22–24], [37] | Separate event, feeling, interpretation and request; show sources; allow rejection | Does correcting an assumption improve subsequent suggestions? |
| Weekly education | [13–20], [27–28] | Reviewed lessons, one chosen practice, explicit outcome, smaller next step when needed | Benefit of this short curriculum and adaptive sequence |
| Difficult-moment options | [22–26] | Offer perspective, an optional reviewed pause exercise, or a chosen helpful memory | Immediate usefulness, unwanted effects and repeat-use outcomes |
| Human-support request | [01–03], [31–32] | User chooses recipient/text and deliberately calls/messages; offer alternatives | Whether users obtain the kind of support they wanted |
| Empathy practice | [29–30] | Ask what is known, what is guessed, and what the person could ask/listen for | Improved empathic accuracy or listening, separately from self-rated confidence |
| Privacy and local AI | [36–37] | Clear data boundary, source links, corrections and manual fallback | Understanding, trust, model quality and actual privacy behavior |

Journaling and gratitude/positive memories should remain voluntary. No study here requires streaks, an EI score, mass contact broadcasts or automatic disclosure. The research does not establish that Monday is the optimal lesson day or that weekly personalization outperforms a fixed curriculum. Those are product choices to test.

## Claims ready for the pitch

**Problem:** “WHO estimates that one in six people globally experiences loneliness.” [01]

**Specific audience:** “In CDC's 2022 survey across 26 states, nearly three in ten adults aged 18–34 lacked consistent needed social and emotional support.” [02]

**Core mechanism:** “A randomized study of 120 adults found that five days of emotion education improved their ability to distinguish negative emotions, with improvement still detectable a month later.” [09]

**Adult digital learning:** “A structured online emotional-intelligence program improved both self-reported and performance-based emotional skills in a randomized trial with 326 analyzed adults.” [14]

**Yale context:** “A Yale-led review of 424 school-based studies found benefits from social and emotional learning across emotional skills, peer relationships and other outcomes.” [19]

**Product description:** “Bearings is designed to turn private reflection into emotional learning: name what you feel, question an assumption, choose a small next step, and return to see what helped.” This describes the proposed feature loop; only show implemented, verified behavior as working.

**Work context:** “AI is changing how we work while understanding ourselves, communicating needs and collaborating remain valuable.” Employer evidence [33] and workplace training evidence [15] support that framing. They do not establish that the app improves career outcomes.

## Questions judges may ask

**Does naming feelings make people feel better?** Affect labeling has laboratory support [12], and granularity has small observational associations with better outcomes [06–07]. Teaching emotional distinctions improved a scenario measure in one trial [09], while a shorter word-learning trial was null overall [10]. Our intended learning benefit must be evaluated separately.

**Does the app reduce loneliness?** We have problem-scale evidence [01–03] and a rationale for facilitating human support [31–32]. We have not measured Bearings' effects on loneliness. A request for a call is an opportunity for contact; it is not equivalent to an organized support program.

**Why AI?** AI can offer tentative source-linked interpretations or connect a saved situation to reviewed material. The scientific case is for emotional-learning practices; AI's additional value over a curated/manual experience remains a testable hypothesis. It should not assert hidden motives, invent success, or replace the writer's authority.

**Does Yale back the app?** Yale conducts relevant emotional-education research [16–19]. Bearings has no established Yale endorsement, partnership or validation.

**How much does EI improve?** There is no single valid percentage transferable to this app. Studies use different constructs and measures; self-report confidence and performance-based ability are not interchangeable. Cite study-specific results with their units.

**Will it increase empathy or creativity?** Those are future hypotheses. Direct empathy-training trials are encouraging [30]. Creativity evidence is largely correlational and much weaker on objective measures [34].

## A realistic evaluation path

Start with usability and correctness: can users reject an AI assumption, see the source, complete a reviewed lesson, record a mixed/unresolved outcome and use manual features when AI is unavailable? Keep fictional demonstration outcomes clearly identified.

For initial product research, compare the emotional-learning flow against an active comparison such as the same private journal with ordinary reflection prompts. Define a primary outcome before collecting results: applying emotional distinctions to new scenarios is closer to the feature's purpose than counting emotion words. Include optional self-reported clarity/usefulness and whether a selected practice was attempted/helpful. Record attrition and negative/unhelpful experiences, and assess persistence at follow-up.

Evaluate AI's contribution separately from the learning content, for example reviewed/manual versus reviewed content with user-correctable AI assistance. Do not assume more usage means more benefit. A small pilot can establish feasibility and identify problems; it cannot credibly establish population-level clinical efficacy. Any study involving actual journal data needs a separate consent/data-handling design rather than adding analytics to the MVP.
